## Forest-level research requirement
Every trace-level finding SHOULD be tested against the whole historical field before promotion:
- does it recur in independent substrates?
- did it migrate from explicit -> learned/latent -> re-externalized form?
- what does the older embodiment make explicit that the newer one hides?
- what does the newer embodiment make flexible/general that the older one hard-coded?
- what failure boundary accompanies each embodiment?
- what candidate causal enabler could explain the transition, and what competing explanation survives?

## Cross-domain transfer requirement
For recurring high-value invariants, explicitly test applicability in:
- code/debugging;
- research/scientific inference;
- planning/agents;
- tool use;
- memory/currentness;
- diagnosis/incident response;
- math/formal verification;
- LBE semantic-field design;
- Standard Uplift dataset design;

A transfer is admitted only when the **consequence/failure structure** matches. Surface analogy is insufficient.

## LHIT transfer law
`LHIT != CONVERSATION FORMAT`

If a long-horizon invariant is useful in code, research, planning, debugging, memory, tool operation, or another domain, re-derive and use it there.

Examples:
- code: earlier state/config becomes causally binding later;
- research: later evidence invalidates an earlier premise and its descendants;
- planning: past actions alter future resource/option topology;
- debugging: earlier failure boundary prevents later isomorphic regressions;
- memory: later implicit evidence changes the applicability of older state.
